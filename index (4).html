Independent study workbook for the SSB interview

Defence awareness you can defend under questioning.

A structured workbook on the Army, Navy, Air Force, joint defence and world security. Each topic gives you research leads and cross-questions, so you learn to explain a fact, weigh the trade-offs and say what you would check.

Read the study planStart a drill

No account. No tracking. Your progress stays in your browser.

The method

  1. ResearchUse the leads to find reliable sources. Write two or three lines in your own words.
  2. ReasonWork the cross-questions: fact, trade-off, alternative, what you would check.
  3. SpeakAnswer aloud against the clock. Rate yourself honestly.
  4. ReviewThe drill brings back what you missed, so weak spots get the time.

    0. How to use this

    One page per topic, always in this order:

    1. What it is (2 lines)
    2. Key facts (numbers marked “approx”)
    3. Why it matters to India
    4. The debate (two sides)
    5. Cross-questions with your own reasoning
    6. To verify

    30-second answer formula (use for every cross-question):
    Fact, then trade-off, then alternative, then what you would check, then your view with a caveat. Never bluff a number. Say “approximately”, “sources vary”, or “I’d need to check”.

    Four lenses to apply to ANY topic (SSB IOs love these):

    • Strategic: what does it do for national security?
    • Economic: what does it cost, who builds it, jobs, exports?
    • Ethical/human: civilians, soldiers’ lives, morality of the action
    • Practical (officer’s view): “You are the commander. What do you do on Monday morning?”

    Answer traps to avoid:

    • Absolute words: “always”, “never”, “100% failure”. Use “largely”, “mostly”.
    • Praising only the Indian side. Show you understand the adversary’s logic.
    • Reciting a Wikipedia list. Give one reason and one example.
    • Taking political sides. Stay with national interest, official position, and the trade-offs.
    • Claiming knowledge you lack. “I haven’t studied that in depth, but my understanding is…”

    Each case file gives background facts and the questions an assessor may ask. Model answers are left out on purpose: build your own, then test it in drill mode.     L1

    The Army’s armoured fleet

    What it is

    the main battle tanks for plains and desert warfare: the T-90S Bhishma, the upgraded T-72 Ajeya and the indigenous Arjun, with the Zorawar light tank and the Future Ready Combat Vehicle (FRCV) planned.

    Key facts (approx)

    total main battle tanks about 3,700 to 4,000 (estimates); T-90S about 1,300 (licence-built in India); T-72 about 2,400, with a life-extension of about 1,200 reported in March 2026 to keep them to around 2040; Arjun Mk1 about 124 plus 118 Mk1A ordered; Zorawar about 25 tonnes, three crew, 59 ordered, for high altitude; FRCV planned at roughly 1,700 to 1,800 tanks over 10 to 15 years. One website reverses the T-90 and T-72 numbers, so give ranges.

    Why it matters

    armoured thrusts and counter-thrusts in Punjab and Rajasthan, and mass as deterrence against Pakistan. In the mountains weight and altitude limit heavy tanks, which is why a light tank exists.

    The debate

    the Arjun is very heavy (about 67 tonnes, a problem for bridges and logistics) versus self-reliance; tanks are vulnerable to drones and anti-tank missiles versus survivability in combined-arms teams; light tanks give mobility but less protection.

    Check before the interview

    • current tank totals
    • Zorawar trials
    • FRCV status

    L2

    Kargil and Operation Vijay (1999)

    What it is

    the Army’s operation to clear Pakistani intrusions on the heights across the LoC in Kargil.

    Key facts (approx)

    intrusions were reported around 3 May 1999; the Battle of Tololing ran roughly 14 May to 7 July; the 155 mm Bofors gun was decisive; the IAF and Navy supported (Safed Sagar and Talwar); India lost about 527 soldiers with over 1,300 wounded (the commonly cited figure); four Param Vir Chakras (Batra, Pandey, Sanjay Kumar, Yogendra Yadav); Kargil Vijay Diwas is 26 July. The Kargil Review Committee was set up on 29 July 1999 and criticised intelligence (human intelligence in particular); the Group of Ministers (2000 to 2001) led to reforms such as the Defence Intelligence Agency and, much later, the CDS post (2019). “Operation Vijay” also names the 1961 Goa action.

    Why it matters

    the first limited war between two nuclear states, fought with a strict no-crossing rule on the LoC.

    The debate

    intelligence failure versus political and institutional failure; whether the no-crossing rule saved India from escalation or cost soldiers’ lives; victory by objectives (all positions regained) versus cost.

    Check before the interview

    • casualty figures and dates for specific battles

    L3

    The aircraft carriers

    What it is

    India’s two carriers, INS Vikramaditya and INS Vikrant, the heart of sea-control planning.

    Key facts (approx)

    Vikramaditya is the modified ex-Soviet Admiral Gorshkov (bought 2004, commissioned 2013, about 44,500 tonnes); Vikrant is the first indigenous carrier (Cochin Shipyard, commissioned 2022, about 45,000 tonnes, about 23,000 crore rupees, ski-jump deck, about 12 MiG-29K in the air wing); the planning idea has been one carrier on each seaboard plus a reserve; an order for 26 Rafale-M was reported in April 2025; a third carrier (Vishal) was described as elusive in April 2026.

    Why it matters

    air cover beyond the range of shore-based aircraft, protection of sea lanes and island territories, humanitarian and diplomatic roles.

    The debate

    carriers are vulnerable to anti-ship missiles versus the value of mobile air power; carriers versus submarines on cost; a fighter shortage limits the value of a third carrier.

    • Rafale-M delivery timeline
    • Vishal status

    L4

    Operation Trident (December 1971)

    What it is

    the Navy’s missile-boat attack on Karachi on the night of 4 to 5 Dec 1971 (hence Navy Day on 4 Dec); Operation Python followed on 8 to 9 Dec.

    Key facts

    three Vidyut-class missile boats (INS Nipat, Nirghat and Veer), each with four Styx missiles, part of the 25th “Killer” Squadron under Cdr Babru Bhan Yadav, with escort ships; PNS Khaibar and the minesweeper PNS Muhafiz were hit and Karachi’s oil installations burned; the attack was a first for missile boats in the Indian Navy and made planners rethink the use of small ships.

    Why it matters

    it showed that small missile boats could hurt a larger fleet, and it pressed the Pakistan Navy back into port.

    The debate

    accounts differ on who deserves credit for the oil-tank fires (the Navy or IAF strikes); the boats’ short range and need for escorts.

    Check before the interview

    • ships sunk and individual officers named

    L5

    The Su-30MKI

    What it is

    the IAF’s long-range twin-engine multirole fighter, of Russian design and built under licence by HAL.

    Key facts (approx)

    derived from the Su-27; first Russian-built aircraft accepted in 2002 and the first India-assembled one in 2004; about 272 contracted with about 259 in service; 12 more ordered in Dec 2024 (about 13,500 crore rupees) at about 62.6% indigenous content, with delivery dates reported variously (late 2026 to FY2028 to 2029); a “Super Sukhoi” upgrade of about 84 aircraft (about 65,000 crore rupees) approved; the IAF has roughly 30 to 31 fighter squadrons against a sanctioned 42 (sources vary).

    Why it matters

    it is about a third of the fighter force and the main platform for range, payload and stand-off weapons.

    The debate

    ageing and serviceability, dependence on Russian spares, upgrade versus new purchase.

    L6

    Operation Safed Sagar (Kargil, 1999)

    What it is

    the IAF’s air operation in the Kargil war, the first large-scale use of air power in Jammu and Kashmir since 1971.

    Key facts

    offensive missions began on 26 May 1999; the IAF was ordered not to cross the LoC; weather and altitude limited it; Mirage 2000s with laser-guided bombs hit bunkers effectively; on 27 May Sqn Ldr Ajay Ahuja died during a reconnaissance sortie (Vir Chakra) and Flt Lt K. Nachiketa was captured and later released; the Navy’s Operation Talwar added pressure by moving the Eastern Fleet to join the Western Fleet in the Arabian Sea.

    Why it matters

    it showed the need for precision weapons, high-altitude operations and better surveillance.

    The debate

    the no-crossing rule (escalation risk against military efficiency); whether air power or artillery and infantry decided the war.

    • aircraft losses and exact dates
